Landing page review

The page argues that description, not action, explains the gaps. Four things on it argue the opposite.

Every criticism below is about the same failure: an element that reads as a verdict on councils when the project's whole claim is that it is measuring language. Each is shown as it stands now, then as I would change it.

01

The map is coloured by a number that rewards filing more reports

With All years selected, a council is shaded by the Goals it evidenced in any report from 2023 to 2025. A council that filed three reports had three chances to evidence each Goal; one that filed once had one. The caption says so. But the caption is 12px and the map is 400px, and colour is read before text is.

The result is that the largest object on the landing page is a league table of reporting frequency, presented in the visual language of performance.

As it stands

Two councils evidencing the same eight Goals per report, shaded by the union across all years.

Identical reporting behaviour. Two shades apart on the map.

What I would do

Shade by one reporting year. Every council on screen is then measured against one report, and the comparison is like for like.

Council A

8 of 17
Council B
2024 · 8
8 of 17

Same behaviour, same shade. The year chips stay, but one is always active — there is no All years.

Also worth

Consider dropping the choropleth entirely and making the map a locator — uniform fill, one dot for the council you searched. The map is genuinely useful for finding a council and actively misleading for ranking them. Right now it is asked to do both.

02

Grey means “no report” and reads as “worst”

South Australia and the Northern Territory are grey on the live map. The legend runs few → many with no fourth state, so grey has no declared meaning and inherits the one at the pale end of the ramp. A reader looking at the NT concludes the NT is failing, when the truth is the dataset does not cover it yet.

The footnote already says 516 of 560 LGAs have an analysed report. That fact belongs in the legend, where the eye is.

What I would do

Ends the ramp at a stated maximum, and gives absence its own line rather than the faintest shade of present.

03

The live chips are fine. The failure mode behind them is not.

Live shows All councils · Urban · Rural, which presumably match the values live data carries. The handoff copy of this page still offers Metro, Regional and Rural, and the sample dataset holds only Metro and Rural — so Regional there is a chip pointing at a value nothing holds. That is drift between the handoff and what shipped, not a live bug.

What the episode exposes is worth fixing anyway: because class is a recognised parameter, a value the data does not hold returns zero councils with no explanation. Browse’s existing “this filter is not supported” notice only fires for unrecognised keys, never unrecognised values. Any stale link, old bookmark or renamed category fails the same silent way.

Live, and correct
All councils Urban Rural
Handoff copy, drifted
Metro → Regional → Rural →
No council matches these filters.

A dead end that reads as a finding about the dataset.

What I would do
Asked for Regional The dataset groups councils as Urban or Rural. Showing all 533 councils instead.
Urban Rural

Browse already has this notice — it just never fires for a bad value on a good key. Extending it costs almost nothing and covers every stale link, not only these chips.

Then generate the chips from the same values Browse filters on, so the two cannot drift again.

04

Findings goes nowhere, and Method goes somewhere that answers a different question

Method resolves to /limitations. Those are two different promises. A reader clicking Method wants to know what the classifier reads, what a threshold is, how an activity gets extracted — the case for trusting the number. Limitations is the case against. Both are worth publishing; under one label, the caveats page has to pretend to be the method page, and the sceptic who came for the mechanism leaves without it.

Findings has no destination at all. An item that never responds teaches people the whole header is decorative, which costs the two links that do work.

As it stands
SDG Alignment Analyser The dataset Findings Method
Method leads to /limitations

One dead item, and one whose label promises the mechanism but delivers the caveats.

What I would do
SDG Alignment Analyser The dataset How it works
How it works leads to What we read → What a match means → What this cannot tell you

Two items, both real. How it works is one page in three parts, ending on the existing Limitations copy — so the caveats keep their home and stop being the answer to “what is your method.” Findings goes until there is a findings page worth the click; the numbers on the landing page are already doing that job.

If you only do one

Change the map to a single year. It is the cheapest of the four and the only one that is currently telling readers something untrue on the page they land on.
