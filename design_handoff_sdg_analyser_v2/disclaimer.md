# Disclaimer and limitations

Three versions of the same statement, for three placements. They must not contradict each
other — if you change the substance, change all three.

Australian English. No emoji, no exclamation. The voice is plain and unhedged: state the limit
directly rather than softening it, because a disclaimer that reads as legal cover invites less
trust than one that reads as a description of the method.

---

## 1. Footer strip — every public page

One line, always visible, 12.5px. This is the only one a casual reader will see.

> This analysis reads what council annual reports **describe**. A Goal with no evidence means
> the report did not describe qualifying work — not that the council did none.
> [About this analysis and its limits →](#limitations)

---

## 2. Short form — method drawer, export cover page, and the top of the limitations page

Three sentences. Use wherever a reader is one click from the numbers.

> **About these results.** The SDG Alignment Analyser is a research tool. It uses automated text
> analysis to match activities described in published council annual reports against the 17 UN
> Sustainable Development Goals. Results are indicative, not authoritative: they describe how a
> council reported its work in a given year, and should not be read as a measure of a council's
> performance, compliance, or contribution to the Goals.

## 3. Full statement — `/limitations`, linked from the footer of every page

> # About this analysis
>
> The SDG Alignment Analyser is a research project. It applies automated text analysis to
> published Australian council annual reports, identifying the activities each report describes
> and matching them against the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals.
>
> Everything on this site is generated by that process. No part of it has been reviewed or
> endorsed by the councils analysed, by the United Nations, or by any government body.
>
> ## What the analysis measures
>
> It measures **language**, not activity. The unit of analysis is a passage of text in an annual
> report, and a match means that passage reads as describing work relevant to a Goal.
>
> A council may do substantial work on a Goal and receive no evidence for it here, simply
> because the report described that work in other terms. The reverse also happens: a passage
> written in the vocabulary of a Goal may be matched to it without describing any real activity.
> We have found both patterns repeatedly.
>
> **A Goal with no evidence is a statement about a document, not about a council.**
>
> ## Known limitations
>
> **Extraction.** Activities are read from prose. Reports written as tables, objective codes or
> status grids yield far fewer described activities than narrative reports — in our data, by a
> factor of three or more between comparable councils. Where this materially affects a result,
> the council's page says so.
>
> **Coverage differs by council and by year.** Not every council has an analysed report for
> every year, and reporting formats change between years. Comparisons across councils or across
> time carry both effects.
>
> **Thresholds are set, not discovered.** A passage is matched to a Goal when its similarity
> score passes a threshold calibrated for that Goal. Different thresholds would produce
> different results. The thresholds in use are published in the method notes.
>
> **Goals are not equally detectable.** Some Goals are expressed in language that overlaps
> heavily with ordinary local-government writing, and are matched readily. Others are expressed
> in terms that rarely appear in council reports at all. A low result for a Goal partly reflects
> this, and not only the work behind it.
>
> **Automated matching makes errors.** Individual matches may be wrong in either direction. Every
> claim on this site is shown alongside the passage that produced it so you can judge it
> yourself, which is the intended way to read these results.
>
> **Source documents.** Analysis is performed on annual reports as published. We do not correct,
> supplement or verify their contents, and errors in a source report carry into the analysis.
>
> ## What this is not
>
> This is not a ranking, a scorecard, an audit or a compliance assessment. We do not publish
> league tables and do not order councils by performance. Where a list is sorted, the sort is a
> convenience and is labelled as such.
>
> Results should not be used to allocate funding, assess a council's performance, or support a
> claim of compliance with any standard or reporting framework.
>
> ## Corrections
>
> If a result appears wrong, tell us. Council officers may request a re-analysis, submit a
> corrected or superseded report, or ask for a specific match to be reviewed. Contact
> [address].
>
> ## Data and licensing
>
> Council annual reports are published documents and remain the copyright of their councils.
> Boundary data is © Commonwealth of Australia (Australian Bureau of Statistics), Australian
> Statistical Geography Standard Edition 3, used under CC BY 4.0.
>
> [Method notes →](#method) · Last updated [date]

## 4. Export agreement — shown when requesting an account type that can export

Stronger than the informational versions above, because an exported file leaves the site and
arrives somewhere with no surrounding context. This is a **gate**: the applicant reads it and
ticks to agree before an officer or admin account is granted.

Present it as body text with four separate checkboxes — not one "I agree to the terms" tick.
Each clause is a distinct commitment and a single tick lets a reader skip all four.

> ## Before you can export
>
> Exported files leave this site and are easily forwarded without the context around them. The
> conditions below apply to every file you export and to any figure you take from one.
>
> **❑ I understand these results are indicative, not authoritative.**
> They are produced by automated text analysis of published annual reports. They have not been
> reviewed or endorsed by the councils analysed, by the United Nations, or by any government
> body. Individual matches may be wrong in either direction.
>
> **❑ I understand these results measure reporting, not performance.**
> A Goal with no evidence means the report did not describe qualifying work — not that the
> council did none. I will not present these figures as a measure of a council's performance,
> compliance, or contribution to the Goals.
>
> **❑ I will not use these results to rank or score councils.**
> This includes league tables, grades, and comparisons that imply one council performs better
> than another. Coverage depends heavily on how a report is written, and reports differ enough
> that such comparisons are not sound.
>
> **❑ I will carry the attribution and the limitations with the figures.**
> If I publish, quote or circulate any figure from an export — including in a slide, briefing or
> media enquiry — I will state that it comes from the SDG Alignment Analyser, name the report
> year it refers to, and link to the limitations statement.
>
> Every export carries this statement in the file itself. Removing it does not remove these
> conditions.
>
> [ Cancel ]  [ Agree and request access ]

### Rules for this gate

- **The registered path is self-service.** Email, password, four ticks, account active — no
  waiting, no approval. Export is not a privilege we grant; the ticks are the condition. Only
  the officer path is reviewed by a human.

- **Record the agreement**: who, when, and the version of this text they saw. If the text
  changes materially, re-prompt on next export.
- **Show it at access request**, not at every export — a per-export dialogue is dismissed
  unread within a week.
- **Do not let it be inherited silently.** An admin granting someone the officer role must have
  that person agree themselves.
- Every export still embeds the short form from section 2, regardless of the agreement. A file
  is the unit that travels; it has to carry its own context.

## Placement checklist

| Where | Which version |
| --- | --- |
| Landing page footer | Footer strip |
| Browse footer | Footer strip |
| Council page footer | Footer strip |
| Method & settings drawer | Short form, at the top |
| Exported PDF / CSV / JSON | Short form, on the cover or as a header comment |
| Requesting an export-capable account | **Export agreement**, four separate ticks, recorded |
| `/limitations` | Full statement |
| Any page stating a national finding | Footer strip, plus the scope wording from `llm-narrative.md` |

Exports are the easiest to forget and the most likely to be forwarded without context — a CSV
that reaches a councillor's inbox with no disclaimer attached is the realistic failure mode.

## Not legal advice

This is a drafting starting point written from what the tool actually does. It is not legal
advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer. Have someone qualified check it before launch,
particularly the corrections and licensing sections.
